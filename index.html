How levels work, and why

This is the whole course from the lecture recordings and PDFs, in the order you'd actually take a trade. Every chart you open comes down to one question: which point is confirmed, and what would break it?

Pre-trade checklist

  1. Monday: D1, then H4, then H1. Mark the last 1-2-3 on each and write down the direction.
  2. Which multi-timeframe condition is it? That tells you which timeframe's levels you trade (part 9).
  3. Is price at a level right now? The level price is sitting at sets today's bias, not your opinion.
  4. Level tapped. Drop to the confirmation timeframe and wait for its CHoCH (normal or 5-wave, whichever comes first).
  5. Wait for a green or red confirmation candle on one of the new CHoCH levels. Engulfing beats everything else.
  6. Is the stop 25 pips or less on gold? If not, skip it or refine on M1.
  7. Is it London or New York session? If not, use half size.
  8. Target is the last confirmed high (for buys) or low (for sells), or an opposing zone if one sits before it. Book 80–90% there, and move the rest to break even.
  9. Two trades a day at most. One win and one loss means you're done.
Read the chart
1

Impulse, correction and the 2-candle retracement

In a bullish market the buying waves are impulse and the selling waves are correction. Bearish is the mirror. You never trade the correction. A correction only counts when:

  • two counter-colour candles print back to back, and
  • the second one's body closes beyond the first one's wick: below its low in a bull leg, above its high in a bear leg.

A wick through doesn't count. Closing past the first body but not its wick doesn't count. Only the first valid retracement in a leg counts.

2

Levels 1, 2, 3

Bullish: the retracement confirms the high. That's level 1. When one candle closes above it, the lowest point between that high and the close becomes the confirmed low, level 2. The newest high, not yet broken, is level 3, the tentative high. Bearish is the mirror: low first (two green candles), then the high confirms when a candle closes below.

Level 2 has a name. In a bull it's the last support in the market (LSM), and in a bear it's the last resistance (LRM). Each time structure moves on, the support moves up with it. That's what support and resistance really are in this method.

Patience rule: once a high is confirmed, ignore new red pairs until price closes above it. Rarely, a single candle confirms both a high and a low.

3

Engulfing retracement (mainly D1)

On the daily chart, waiting for two candles can cost you days, so one candle can count as the retracement if both checks pass:

  • it's a genuinely big-bodied engulfing candle compared with the candles around it (wicks don't matter, the body does), and
  • its body closes beyond the previous candle's low (bull structure) or high (bear structure).

A small candle engulfing another small candle doesn't count. The course calls these low-probability on minor pairs, so treat them as a backup method, not the main one.

4

Change of character (CHoCH)

The trend flips when two candles close beyond level 2, the LSM or LRM. Colour doesn't matter: red-red or red-green both count. The trend changes on the close of the second candle.

Fake-out: one candle closes beyond it and the very next candle closes back inside. That's not a shift. A BOS needs one close. A CHoCH needs two.

After a CHoCH, start marking structure again from the double top or double bottom.

5

The three CHoCH levels

The last 1-2-3 before the shift turns into your three entry levels. After a bearish CHoCH:

  • A+ / QML: level 1, the older high that got broken. Smallest stop, best reward. It only becomes active once the CHoCH is confirmed.
  • SBR: level 2, the broken support, now resistance.
  • Double top: level 3. It plays least often.

Draw zones from the wick: top wick for a high, bottom wick for a low, or the body if there's no wick. Bullish is the mirror: QML, RBS, double bottom. A level is spent once it has played, and it's invalid once two candles close through it. The course says to trade every level, not only A+, because sometimes only the SBR plays.

6

5-wave shift (ISS): the early CHoCH

A normal CHoCH can take weeks on higher timeframes. The shortcut: inside the last impulse, from level 2 to level 3, on the same timeframe, if price builds 5 waves against the trend (using the same 2CR and 1-close rules), structure has shifted early.

Trade from level 3 and level 4 of those five waves. Level 4 is the A+. Use it when price is ranging between level 2 and level 3. Whichever comes first, normal CHoCH or 5 waves, is your CHoCH.

7

Dual CHoCH (rare, H1/H4/D1 only)

Bullish, then bearish, then bullish again. After a bearish CHoCH, price reverses from an exact zone and two candles close beyond the double top. Your three sell levels flip into buy levels, and the reversal point becomes a 4th level. It happens a few times a year at most, so don't go looking for it.

Where to trade
8

Trend-joining levels (TGL)

In a running trend, the last 1-2-3 gives you two with-trend entries. TGL1 is the wick of level 1, and TGL2 is the wick of level 2 (the LSM/LRM), which is the A+. Use TGL2 in normal conditions. Use TGL1 when the market is strongly trending or volatile and may not come back to TGL2. If price closes beyond TGL2, that's a CHoCH, and you switch to part 5. The course rates TGLs at roughly 7–8 out of 10 levels playing.

9

Multi-timeframe: the three conditions

Do it on Monday for the week. Start on D1, then H4, then H1, marking the last 1-2-3 on each. D1's direction is the trend.

Mirror for sells. Bias on any given day comes from the level price is sitting at: at a sell level you look to sell, at a buy level you look to buy. Counter-trend reactions from lower levels are smaller, so expect less from them.

10

Confluence (optional, about 20% of the method)

  • Demand/supply: the opposite-colour candle right before an impulse. For demand, mark the red candle from open to low. For supply, mark the green candle from open to high. Best on H1–D1. Only about 4–5 in 10 play, so it's strongest when it overlaps a TGL2.
  • FVG: a 3-candle gap. Use the first one to the left of price. Never trade it on its own.
  • Fibonacci: pull it across the last 1-2-3. A level that sits inside the 0.5–0.618 golden zone gets a little more trust.
  • Liquidity and inducement: equal highs or lows (several wicks at one price) are a pool of stops. Inducement is a range, then support and resistance forming, then a fake move that sweeps those stops just before price taps your level. Trade a sweep only with an M1 CHoCH. Entering on the first candle after the sweep means a 30–50 pip stop. A level that has already played becomes liquidity, not an entry.
  • Order blocks, breaker blocks and POIs are supply and demand under different names. You don't need them.
  • Gold: expect a pullback after roughly every $10 move, and reactions at round numbers in multiples of 5.

After the lower-timeframe CHoCH, mark its three levels and wait for a green candle (buy) or red candle (sell) on one of them. Bullish/bearish engulfing is the best confirmation, then morning/evening star, then hanging man. A level on its own is a 50/50, and the course says confirmation lifts that to about 80/20. Tap-and-go level: an M15 or H1 buy or sell zone with an FVG inside it. Price often reacts straight off the tap. You can enter on the first M1 candle with a 20–25 pip stop (risky), or wait for the M1 CHoCH (safe). If the reaction is too fast to catch, take the retest, the sweep, or the FVG. Never mark levels on M1 or M5. Those timeframes are only for confirmation.

12

When and how to trade

The course gives times in IST, so convert them to your zone. Don't look at charts before 10:00. Do your analysis 10:00–11:30. Trade London 11:30–14:00. From 14:00–17:00, take no new entries (an open trade can stay open). Trade New York 17:00–20:30, with no new entries after about 20:00. After 20:30, close anything that isn't at least 100 pips in profit, because overnight spreads widen and can take out your stop. If a setup comes outside the windows, use half size.

  • Only trade at your desk, never from your phone on the go. Don't leave pending orders sitting while you're away.
  • Pairs: the majors plus GBPJPY. Avoid exotics. Avoid broker deposit "bonuses".
  • If price is stuck in a range between levels, do nothing until it closes out of the range.
  • Learn on H1 and H4. The instructor traded H1 and above for about two and a half years before going down to M15 and M5. He also lost about $10,000 in one news spike trading 8 lots at an A+, because size kills you faster than direction.
Manage the trade
13

Stop, target, exits

  • Stop: below the confirmation candle or the refined zone, plus spread. On gold that's 20–25 pips, 30–35 at the very most. If the stop would be bigger, wait for an M1 CHoCH for a tighter entry, or skip the trade.
  • Target: for a buy, the last confirmed high on the trade's timeframe. For a sell, the last confirmed low. If an opposing zone (supply, or a sell level) sits before that, take the first one. The simple version is 1:2. On gold, expect a pullback after roughly every $10 move.
  • Exit: book 80–90% at the target and move the rest to break even.
  • Risk-free move: at 1:1, close 50% of the position. If the stop is then hit, you lose nothing.
  • Size by conviction: full size on an A+, half size on SBR/RBS, the double top/bottom, or TGL1.
  • The course prefers one 100-pip trade over five 20-pip trades: "5 trades, 5 different psychologies."
14

Risk and lot size

Two trades a day, maximum: one in London and one in New York.

  • If the first trade wins, the second risks only 30–40% of that profit.
  • If the first trade loses, the second trade uses half size.
  • One win and one loss means you stop for the day. Two losses means you stop for the day. You never try to get back to break even.

The course's gold lot sizes are 0.02 for a $100 account, 0.05 for $500 and 0.10 for $1,000. With a 25-pip stop, that's about 5%, 2.5% and 2.5% of the account per trade. The general formula is lot = account × risk % ÷ (stop pips × pip value). For gold on the calculator, the course says to choose EURUSD.

The course also says 5–10% per trade counts as "low risk", and that smaller accounts should risk more. Don't do this. At 5% risk, ten losses in a row (which will happen) cut your account by about 40%. Use 0.5–1% while you're on demo and in your first live months.

16

Psychology

The course's psychology lecture comes down to this. Fear comes in two kinds: "will the strategy work?" and fear of losses. The cure is patience (no trade until price reaches your level), discipline (show up for both sessions, because missed sessions skew your results) and consistency. Judge the method over 100 trades, not 10. A run of 6 losses inside 100 trades is normal.

  • Decide your daily target before the session, and size it in pips. For example, $100 from 50 pips means 0.20 lot. Then stop when you hit it.
  • Overtrading to win back a $20–30 loss is what wrecked the instructor's early accounts. After two or three trades, log off. If watching charts has become a habit, delete the app each night.
  • "It's not how often you're right. It's how much you make when you're right and how much you lose when you're wrong."
  • Withdraw part of your profit and leave the rest in the account to compound.
  • Going full time: only when one month of trading profit equals six months of salary, and that has held for six months in a row.
  • Learn on currency pairs, not just gold. The course says gold teaches only about half of how structure works. Backtest from 2024 onward.

Parts of the course to ignore

  • 5–10% risk per trade, and "$500 a week using 0.20 lot". See part 14.
  • "75–85% win rate on A+." That's the instructor's claim. Your own 100-trade log is the only number that counts.
  • Withdrawal tips: keeping bank withdrawals under $500 so the bank doesn't notice, and cash-for-USDT through local dealers. Splitting transfers to avoid bank scrutiny can be illegal in itself. Withdraw through official channels and pay the tax.
  • The course is taught on gold with Indian session times. Convert the times to your own time zone, and expect pips to mean different distances on other pairs.
